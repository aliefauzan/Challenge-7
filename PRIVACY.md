# ScamShield privacy policy

Last updated: 17 October 2026

ScamShield checks web pages on your device. It does not collect, send or sell
any information about you or the pages you visit.

## What leaves your device

Only one thing. About twice a day, ScamShield may download its list of scam
warning signs (the "ruleset") from a fixed web address. This is a plain
download of the same file for everyone. The request carries no information
about you, your browsing or your settings. The file is signed, and ScamShield
ignores any copy whose signature does not match.

In builds where no download address is set, ScamShield makes no network
requests at all.

Some warnings have links, such as "Report a mistake" or "How to spot a scam".
If you tap one, it opens a normal web page in your browser. "Report a mistake"
fills in the name of the website that was flagged, such as example.com, so
you can tell us about it. You see the report before you send it. Nothing
else about you is added to these links.

## What stays on your device

| What | Where | How long |
|---|---|---|
| The ruleset and when it was last updated | Extension local storage | Until the next update or until you remove ScamShield |
| Your settings (which checks are on) | Extension sync storage. If you use browser sync, your browser copies these three switches to your other devices | Until you change them |
| The result of the check for each open tab | Extension session storage | Until the tab closes or the browser quits |
| Websites where you closed a banner or chose to continue | Extension session storage | Until the browser quits |

ScamShield does not store the text of the pages you visit, your browsing
history, or anything you type.

## Permissions

ScamShield asks to read websites so that it can look for warning signs on
every page you open. That check happens inside your browser. What it reads
is not saved or sent anywhere.

## App Store privacy label

Data Not Collected. The developer does not collect any data from this app or
its Safari extension.
